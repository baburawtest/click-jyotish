Kartik Purnima is the full-moon day of the Hindu month of Kartik. It is also associated with Dev Diwali, or Dev Deepawali, which is especially well known in Varanasi. For a family prayer, a small diya at home or a temple visit, first confirm the date **for your city** and follow the tradition your household actually observes.

For **New Delhi, Kartik Purnima and Dev Diwali fall on Tuesday, 24 November 2026**. This date is listed in [Drik Panchang's New Delhi Kartik Purnima calendar](https://www.drikpanchang.com/purnima/kartik/kartik-purnima-date-time.html?lang=en&year=2026) and its [day Panchang for 24 November](https://www.drikpanchang.com/panchang/day-panchang.html?date=24%2F11%2F2026&geoname-id=1261481). The article intentionally does not give a universal puja or fasting time: tithi boundaries and local practice need more care than a one-line national timetable can provide.

## Kartik Purnima and Dev Diwali: what is the difference?

“Purnima” refers to a lunar full-moon tithi. Kartik Purnima is therefore a **calendar day**, while customs associated with it can differ from place to place. Families may mark the day with prayer, visiting a place of worship, giving, lighting a lamp or simply taking time for reflection. No single online checklist describes every family's observance.

Dev Diwali, also called Dev Deepawali, is a **festival associated with this Purnima**, particularly with Kashi (Varanasi). The Government of India's [Incredible India guide to Dev Deepawali](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/uttar-pradesh/illuminating-the-eternal-spirit-of-kashi) describes the lights, ghats and cultural setting of the Varanasi celebration. This does not mean a family in Delhi must reproduce a large public festival at home. The place and scale are different; a small, thoughtful observance is still meaningful to those who choose it.

The same date is also listed for **Guru Nanak Jayanti** in the New Delhi Panchang. That is a distinct Sikh observance with its own traditions. Sharing a calendar date does not make these celebrations interchangeable, so it is best to name and respect each one separately.

## Why might another calendar show a different time?

A lunar tithi does not start at midnight. It can begin on one civil date and end on another, and different observances may use different sunrise, evening or community rules. That is why a social-media graphic showing just “24 November” cannot answer every question about a vrat, a visit or a ceremony. The date above is a **New Delhi reference**, not a promise that every city or lineage will use identical timings.

If you live outside Delhi, begin with [Click Jyotish's city-selectable daily Panchang](/#panchang) and then confirm with a trusted local calendar, temple or family elder. Avoid using a precise muhurt from a different location simply because it appears first in search.

## A simple, safe way to prepare at home

An observance need not depend on elaborate shopping or fear of “doing it wrong.” Consider this small plan and adapt it to your own practice:

1. **Choose your intention.** Decide whether the day is for a familiar prayer, remembrance, gratitude, charity or time together. That choice is more useful than collecting a long list of unfamiliar rituals.
2. **Prepare a clean, calm space.** Tidy the place where you normally pray or sit quietly. Set aside only the items your family already uses. If you plan to light a diya, keep it on a stable, heat-safe surface away from curtains, leaves and children.
3. **Check the local date before planning a visit.** Temples and community groups may publish their own programme. Confirm opening hours and arrangements with them directly rather than relying on last year's poster.
4. **Keep shared spaces clean.** If you visit a riverfront or public place, follow local safety advice and leave no plastic, foil or other waste. A respectful observance should also respect the people and environment around it.

Some people associate Kartik Purnima with a vrat or bathing tradition. These are personal and community practices, not requirements for every reader. If a fast is part of your tradition, use the guidance you normally follow and take your own health circumstances seriously. This article is not a fasting prescription.

## If you are visiting Varanasi for Dev Deepawali

Varanasi's public celebration is very different from a quiet home observance. The ghats can draw large crowds, and event logistics can change. Before travelling, check current official travel and crowd-management information, allow extra time and follow on-site safety directions. Do not assume that a photograph or an old video gives you this year's schedule. The [Ministry of Tourism's event listing](https://utsav.gov.in/view-event/dev-deepawali-1) is useful background on the festival; practical arrangements should still be checked close to your visit.

In Delhi, the day can be observed through a local temple, an existing family custom or a simple lamp and moment of reflection at home.

## What follows this day?

Kartik Purnima follows the sequence of [Devutthana Ekadashi and Tulasi Vivah](/blog/devutthana-ekadashi-2026-tulasi-vivah-guide/). Seeing the dates together helps make sense of a busy Kartik calendar: one observance does not cancel another, and different families may emphasize different parts of the season. If you are planning a personal ceremony, use a city-specific Panchang and the guidance of the person conducting it, rather than treating a general article as an exact muhurt service.

The most useful preparation is modest: verify your local date, understand which observance you mean, and choose a form of participation that fits your family and community. That leaves space for devotion without unnecessary confusion.
