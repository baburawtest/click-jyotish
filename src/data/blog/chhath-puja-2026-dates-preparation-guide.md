Chhath Puja is a deeply respected festival of Surya Upasana—devotion to the Sun. It is especially rooted in Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern Uttar Pradesh and Nepal, while today it is observed by families across India and around the world.

In 2026, Chhath Puja is listed from **Friday, 13 November to Monday, 16 November**. The four days bring together discipline, gratitude, cleanliness, family participation and reverence for nature. Exact local timings for Arghya, sunrise and sunset differ by city, so they should always be confirmed from a reliable location-specific Panchang or local community arrangement.

## What makes Chhath Puja special?

Chhath is also known as Surya Shashti. It honours the life-giving presence of the Sun and is marked by offerings to the setting Sun as well as the rising Sun. Bihar Tourism describes it as a four-day observance associated with ritual purity, restraint and devotion.

For many families, the heart of Chhath is not display or decoration. It is preparation with care: keeping the home and offerings clean, sharing responsibilities, respecting the Vrat of the devotee, and creating a peaceful atmosphere.

Each family and community may follow its own customs. If you are participating for the first time, it is best to learn directly from an elder, a local Chhath committee, or the family observing the Vrat.

## Chhath Puja 2026: the four-day flow

### Day 1: Nahay Khay — Friday, 13 November

The observance begins with bathing, cleaning the home and preparing simple sattvic food. This day sets the tone of cleanliness and sincerity for the days that follow.

A practical preparation is to avoid last-minute shopping. Make a simple list for fruits, grains, earthen lamps, bamboo baskets, sugarcane and other family-specific Puja items. Buy only what is genuinely needed and avoid waste.

### Day 2: Kharna — Saturday, 14 November

Kharna is associated with a day-long fast that is traditionally concluded in the evening after worship. Families prepare Prasad according to their custom, often including kheer and simple offerings.

This can be a busy day. Keep the kitchen calm and organised, and let the person observing the Vrat rest where possible. If you are a family member, practical support—cleaning, arranging ingredients, checking transport or helping with children—can be more meaningful than giving advice.

### Day 3: Sandhya Arghya — Sunday, 15 November

The third day includes preparation for the evening offering, commonly called Sandhya Arghya. Devotees and families gather near a ghat, riverbank, pond or designated community water body to offer prayers to the setting Sun.

If you are attending a public ghat, plan travel early. Follow local safety instructions, keep pathways clear, carry drinking water for accompanying family members, and avoid adding plastic waste to the site. Respect photography boundaries; for many devotees this is a personal and sacred moment.

### Day 4: Usha Arghya — Monday, 16 November

The final day takes place before or around sunrise, when offerings are made to the rising Sun. Afterwards, the Vrat is concluded and Prasad is shared with family, neighbours and friends.

Because this is an early morning observance, prepare clothes, offerings and transport the night before. A calm departure is safer and more respectful than rushing at dawn.

## A simple, respectful Chhath preparation checklist

- Confirm your family’s dates, items and local ghat plan.
- Check city-specific sunrise information only from a reliable local source.
- Keep offerings and the Puja area clean and simple.
- Use reusable baskets and minimise disposable plastic.
- Arrange safe travel for elders and children.
- Respect fasting choices; do not pressure anyone to observe the Vrat.
- Follow local ghat rules, crowd guidance and water-safety instructions.
- Treat Chhath as a time for gratitude and service, not fear-based predictions.

## A note for families outside Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh

Chhath has travelled far beyond its regional roots. If you are joining friends, neighbours or a community celebration, approach it with humility. Ask before participating in rituals, follow the organiser’s guidance, and avoid presenting one family’s custom as the only correct method.
