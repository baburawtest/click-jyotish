“Which hand should I show?” is one of the first questions people ask before a Palmistry or Hastarekha reading. The short answer is that both palms can be useful. If you are showing only one clear photo, start with your active or dominant hand—the one you normally use for writing or detailed work.

Palmistry traditions are not identical. Some older customs use different starting hands for men and women; other readers place more importance on the hand used most actively in daily life. A careful reader should explain the approach rather than claim there is only one universal rule.

## Start with the hand you use most

For a practical online reading, begin with the hand you use most naturally. For many people this is the right hand; for left-handed people it may be the left. If you use both hands equally, choose the one you use for writing, signing or other fine work, then share the other hand as well if requested.

In modern Palmistry language, the active hand is often discussed as the hand connected with the choices, habits and direction a person is shaping. The other hand may be compared as a traditional reference point for natural tendencies. These are reflective ideas, not fixed facts or guaranteed predictions.

## Why both palms may be useful

Comparing both hands can give a reader a fuller visual starting point. The lines may be clearer on one palm, and lighting or camera angle can make a genuine line look different in a photograph. A personal Palmistry discussion is not only about a single line; it also includes your question, the clarity of the image and the wider context you choose to share.

## How to take a useful palm photo

Good preparation matters more than trying to decide every line yourself.

1. Open your hand comfortably, with the whole palm and fingers visible.
2. Use daylight or a bright, even room. Avoid harsh shadows.
3. Keep the camera directly above the palm and hold it steady.
4. Remove filters and avoid jewellery that covers the hand.
5. Take one photo of the dominant hand and, if asked, one of the other hand.

Do not upload identity documents, another person’s hand without permission, or images you would not want handled by an online service.

## A note about traditional rules

You may hear different answers such as “right hand for men” or “left hand for women.” These are traditional starting customs found in some systems. They do not mean that the lines themselves have one fixed meaning for every person. A practical dominant-hand approach is easier to explain, respects left-handed and ambidextrous people, and keeps the focus on your actual question.

## Choose reflection over certainty

Palmistry is best approached as spiritual and personal guidance. It can encourage reflection on patterns, strengths and next steps, but it cannot reliably determine medical outcomes, legal decisions, financial results, pregnancy, lifespan or a guaranteed date for an event.

If you would like a private discussion rather than a generic online prediction, start with your main question and a clear photo.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I am left-handed?

Show your left hand first if it is the hand you normally use for writing and detailed work. If possible, provide both palms so they can be compared clearly.

### Is a free palm image tool the same as a personal reading?

No. A quick image tool can only offer a broad reflection from the visible photo. A personal reading considers clear images, both palms when helpful, and the question you want to explore.
