If you do not know your exact birth time, you are not alone. Many people only know the date and city of birth, or have a family memory such as “early morning” or “after sunset.” That is still useful information—but it should be handled honestly.

The simple answer is: a conversation can begin without an exact birth time, but not every part of a Janam Kundli should be treated as equally certain. A thoughtful consultation explains the limit instead of filling in a convenient time and presenting the result as precise.

## What changes when the time is uncertain?

In traditional Kundli work, the birth time helps establish the rising sign and the houses. Those details can change as the day moves forward. If the time is uncertain, it is sensible to be cautious about conclusions that depend strongly on a particular Ascendant, house placement or very specific event timing.

That does not mean the consultation has no value. It means the scope changes. A responsible astrologer can discuss the information that can be considered with more care, explain what remains uncertain, and tell you whether more birth-time research would be useful before making a detailed judgement.

## Start by looking for a better time record

Before booking, spend a little time checking the sources closest to the day of birth:

- A birth certificate, hospital record or old family diary may note a time.
- A parent, grandparent or older relative may remember whether it was before sunrise, around a meal, after office hours or late at night.
- An older Janam Patrika may include the time that was used when it was prepared.
- If you only have an estimate, write it as an estimate. “Around 7 pm” is more useful than pretending it was exactly 7:00 pm.

Do not share identity documents, bank information, passwords or private family records that are not needed for the discussion.

## What to prepare for a focused consultation

Bring the three basics you know: date of birth, place of birth and the best available time information. Then choose one main question. It may relate to career direction, a job decision, a relationship concern, education or a period of personal change.

One clear question is more useful than a long list of worries. It helps the consultation stay practical and lets Deepak explain which parts of the discussion are based on reliable birth details and which need caution.

## Avoid a false sense of precision

Some online tools create a chart even when a default noon time is used. Such a chart can be a starting point for learning, but it should not be presented as proof of a precise Ascendant or exact timing. If the birth time is missing, say so at the beginning of the consultation.

This is especially important for decisions involving health, law, money, safety or mental wellbeing. Jyotish can be used for personal reflection and spiritual perspective; it should not replace qualified professional advice or your own informed judgement.

## When a personal discussion is the better next step

If the question matters to you and the time is uncertain, a personal conversation is often more helpful than a generic automated report. You can explain what you know, ask what information matters, and decide whether a fuller Kundli analysis is suitable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an approximate birth time better than no time?

It can be useful if it is clearly labelled as approximate. A time remembered as “around morning prayer” is not the same as a time recorded by a hospital, so the interpretation should reflect that difference.

### Can a name alone give the exact Chandra Rashi?
