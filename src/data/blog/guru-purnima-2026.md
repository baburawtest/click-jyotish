Guru Purnima is observed on Ashadha Purnima and is also known as Vyasa Purnima. It is a meaningful pause to remember the teachers, elders and experiences that have shaped our path.

## Why Guru Purnima matters

The day is traditionally connected with Maharshi Veda Vyasa and with the larger idea of the guru: someone who helps turn confusion into understanding. A guru may be a spiritual teacher, parent, mentor, book, or even a difficult life lesson that brings maturity.

## A simple, sincere way to observe

1. Begin the day with a calm bath and a few minutes of silence.
2. Offer a prayer, flowers or heartfelt gratitude to your chosen deity, guru or teacher.
3. Call or message a teacher, parent or mentor who has helped you.
4. Read one page from a meaningful text, or make a small commitment to learning.
5. Give dakshina, donate books, or help someone learn according to your ability.

## A Jyotish perspective

In Vedic tradition, Guru (Jupiter) represents wisdom, counsel, expansion and ethical understanding. Rather than treating the day as a shortcut for instant results, use it to strengthen your relationship with learning and right guidance.

For a personal puja or any time-sensitive practice, confirm the relevant local panchang and follow your family tradition.
