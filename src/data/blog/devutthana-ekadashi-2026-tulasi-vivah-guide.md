Devutthana Ekadashi is an important observance in the Hindu month of Kartik. Also called Prabodhini or Dev Uthani Ekadashi, it marks, in Vaishnava tradition, the end of Lord Vishnu’s four-month period of *yogic rest*. For many households, it is a day for prayer, reflection and renewing familiar devotional practices. It also begins a period associated with Tulasi Vivah and the approach of Kartik Purnima.

For **New Delhi**, [Drik Panchang lists Devutthana Ekadashi](https://www.drikpanchang.com/ekadashis/prabodhini/prabodhini-ekadashi-date-time.html?geoname-id=1261481&year=2026) on **Friday, 20 November 2026**. It lists a separate **Gauna or Vaishnava Ekadashi on Saturday, 21 November**. [Tulasi Vivah is listed](https://www.drikpanchang.com/festivals/tulasi-vivah/tulasi-vivah-date-time.html?year=2026) for **Saturday, 21 November**. These distinctions matter: the calendar date followed by one family or temple may differ from another’s practice. Check the Panchang for your city and follow your established family or temple tradition.

## Why are two Ekadashi dates shown?

A *tithi* does not begin or end at midnight like a civil calendar day. Ekadashi observance is determined using the lunar tithi and sunrise-based rules, which can result in a standard observance and a separate Gauna or Vaishnava observance on consecutive days. A date printed on a general festival list therefore should not be treated as a universal fasting instruction.

This is particularly useful to remember when a social-media post announces just one date without naming a city or tradition. If you observe Ekadashi according to a particular temple, *sampradaya* or family custom, use that calendar. If you are simply looking for the day’s significance, it is enough to understand why different dates may appear; there is no need to turn the difference into a cause for worry.

## What is the connection with Tulasi Vivah?

Tulasi Vivah is a ceremonial marriage of the revered Tulasi plant with Lord Vishnu, often represented by Shaligram, or with Krishna in some traditions. The observance is associated with the days from Devutthana Ekadashi toward Kartik Purnima, although the customary date and form of celebration vary. Drik Panchang describes [regional differences in Tulasi Vivah](https://www.drikpanchang.com/festivals/tulasi-vivah/tulasi-vivah-date-time.html?year=2026) across homes and temples.

Some households arrange a wedding-like celebration with decorations, songs and relatives. Others keep it quiet: a clean space around the Tulasi plant, a lamp, flowers and a short prayer. Neither approach needs to be presented as the only “correct” one. Meaningful observance can reflect the time, resources and customs of the household.

## A simple preparation checklist

Start by confirming the **city and date**. Panchang details are location-specific, and a New Delhi date should not be copied into a guide for every city. If your family follows a temple calendar, check its Ekadashi guidance too.

Next, look after the Tulasi plant and its surroundings. Clean the area gently and make sure any decoration will not damage the plant. If you intend to light a diya, place it safely away from leaves, fabric and children. Gather only what your custom calls for; there is no need to purchase an elaborate set of items because a checklist online says so.

If you plan a Tulasi Vivah gathering, agree on a simple order with those taking part. Decide whether you will sing a bhajan, offer flowers, read a familiar prayer or invite a priest to guide a more formal ceremony. A small household observance and a temple-led ritual have different requirements, so avoid treating a short internet guide as a substitute for the instructions of the person conducting your ceremony.

Finally, leave room for regional practice. Traditions in Braj, Maharashtra, Gujarat and South India may differ in their songs, offerings and ceremonial details. Ask elders what your family has done before. Those memories are often more useful than a rigid, one-size-fits-all list.

## What comes after Devutthana Ekadashi?

The Kartik observances do not end with Tulasi Vivah. In New Delhi, [Kartik Purnima falls on Tuesday, 24 November 2026](https://www.drikpanchang.com/purnima/kartik/kartik-purnima-date-time.html?lang=en&year=2026). Dev Diwali and several other observances are also associated with that date. For readers planning to follow the full sequence, it helps to see Ekadashi, Tulasi Vivah and Purnima as related calendar moments rather than as unrelated headlines.

## A thoughtful way to observe

A festival guide should make the day easier to understand, not make a reader anxious about doing everything perfectly. Confirm the date for your place, respect the practice you follow and choose an observance appropriate to your household. If you do not know a ritual, ask a trusted elder or temple rather than relying on a dramatic short video.
